# M17 independent security review for gate G3.01 (FIG-1191)

Reviewer: an AI agent (Claude) that did not write the code under review. Date: 11 Nov 2026. Branch: `makeover/m17-closeout`.

**What this is, and is not.** This is a code-reading and test-writing review by an AI agent. It is not a penetration test, it ran no browser, no live server and no outside network call, and it does not replace Michael's approval of the gate. The builders' own notes (`docs/M17_GATE_G3_01.md`, `docs/M17_LANE_*.md`) were read as claims to be checked, not as evidence. Nothing in `src/` or `prisma/` was changed by this review.

## 1. Result at a glance

## 2. What was checked, tried, and found sound

### 2.1 Support access (FIG-1183)

Read: the whole migration (`20261121110000_support_access`: state-machine trigger, `support_access_read`, the request/open/end functions, grants), `src/services/supportAccessService.ts`.

Tried, and the defence held (tests in `tests/g301ReviewSupportAccess.test.ts`):

- A read in every state short of ACTIVE (PENDING, APPROVED not opened), by the requesting admin and by another platform admin: refused. A session belongs to the admin who opened it: another admin holding the grant id gets nothing. Replay after Management ends the session, after the window passes (checked by the database clock with no job having run), before the window starts: refused.
- Another admin cannot open someone else's request; denied, ended and expired grants cannot be opened; an approval older than an hour cannot be opened.
- A grant id of organisation A never returns B's records; the page-view row lands in A's log only; B's Management cannot decide A's grant (not found).
- Only Management decides. Sales is refused, and so is a custom role holding every grantable key (`security.manage` is reserved).
- Masking is structural. A planted note, deal value, website, custom field, task title and unmasked email or phone appear in none of the five resources, at any page size. Unmasking contact details is its own approval and still shows no money, note or custom field.
- The database clamps the page size to 50 and a negative offset to 0, and refuses an unknown, mixed-case, injected or out-of-scope kind.
- The platform role cannot read or write the grant table or the page-view log, cannot become "the platform actor" by setting the flag itself, and cannot approve. The tenant role cannot create, open, extend, rewrite, scope-widen or delete a grant, cannot edit or delete the page-view log, and cannot re-decide a decided request. One organisation's context cannot see or change another's grants through SQL.

Findings: S-1, S-2, I-6, I-7.

### 2.2 E-signature (FIG-1186)

Read: `quoteSignatureService.ts`, `lib/quoteSignature.ts`, `repositories/quoteSignatures.ts`, `repositories/quoteShares.ts`, the public routes, `quoteDocumentService.ts` (link creation), the `quote_signing` and `quote_guards_strict` migrations. Tests in `tests/g301ReviewSignature.test.ts`.

Held: 256-bit random token, only its SHA-256 stored, the stored hash is not a working link; guessing, truncating, extending or re-casing a token gives one identical 404; a quote link without signing cannot sign. Eight simultaneous signers produce exactly one signature, one acceptance, one timeline entry and one audit entry; a replay answers 409 and changes nothing. The signed document is the one that was sent (hash equal, seal verifies), and the database refuses edits to a sent version, its lines, its document or its signing flag. An expired link cannot be revived (the clock is the server's; unknown body fields are refused), and an organisation moved below V2 or suspended after sending is dead. The public page carries no cost, margin, staff ids or other quotes; a hostile product name is escaped in the client document with a strict content-security-policy; no cross-origin read headers, no cookies, no caching, no referrer. Malformed or oversized bodies give 4xx with no internals. The stored client address is the one the trusted proxy appended, never one the signer wrote; the per-address limit (60 a minute) cannot be dodged by a new first `X-Forwarded-For` entry on this route.

Findings: E-1, G-1, I-2.

### 2.3 API tokens and the public API (FIG-805)

Read: `auth/apiToken.ts`, `apiTokenService.ts`, `api/publicApi/handlers.ts` and schemas, the routes, `lib/idempotency.ts`. Tests in `tests/g301ReviewPublicApi.test.ts`.

Held: token entropy and hash-only storage; the stored hash is not a credential; Bearer only (not a query string, cookie, Basic, extra spaces or other case); one 401 body for every kind of wrong token; revocation, owner removal, role change (Management token whose owner becomes Delivery loses lead creation on the next request), and an organisation moved below V2 all take effect at once. Masking: a role without deal-value rights gets no value from any list, read, search, filter, sort, timeline or PATCH answer, including a custom role with value hidden and `deals.edit.all`. A search by the value finds nothing (no oracle) and deals take no `sort` by value. Tenant isolation: bodies naming another organisation or another organisation's member change nothing and link nothing; contacts, leads and tasks refuse another organisation's company; idempotency keys are private to the person and the organisation. Error bodies carry no stack, SQL or driver text for malformed ids, cursors, limits, sorts, filters, custom-field filters and bad JSON. Token creation refuses unknown scopes, the empty set, more than 25 live tokens, and the plain token is in no list, audit entry or log.

Findings: A-1, SC-1, P-1, I-4, I-5.

### 2.4 Webhooks (FIG-804)

Read: `lib/ssrfGuard.ts`, `lib/webhookEvents.ts`, `webhookService.ts`, `lib/secretBox.ts`. Tests in `tests/g301ReviewSsrf.test.ts` (no database, no outside host) and `tests/g301ReviewWebhooks.test.ts`.

SSRF held against 50 hostile spellings checked by text: octal, hex, whole-number and trailing-dot loopback; full-width and ideographic characters; the metadata address in decimal, hex, IPv4-mapped, NAT64 and 6to4 forms; AWS IPv6 metadata; userinfo, fragment and query tricks; `localhost` variants and internal suffixes; plain `http`, other schemes, privileged ports and credentials. A host name that resolves to any internal address, alone or mixed with a public one, is refused. At connect time the address is judged by the connection's own `lookup`, so a rebinding answer is refused and nothing is sent (tested with internal answers against a loopback stub that records connections: zero). An endpoint whose stored URL was changed to an internal address in the database is refused at delivery before any connection. Note that `https://hooks.example.com\@127.0.0.1/` is parsed by the URL standard as host `hooks.example.com` with a path; the check and the connection read the same parse, so it is not a bypass.

Signing held: HMAC-SHA256 over `timestamp.body`, constant-time compare, a replay outside five minutes and a future stamp are refused, a re-stamped body fails, every attempt is signed afresh, a rotated secret signs the next delivery and the old one no longer verifies. The secret is shown once, stored sealed, and is in no list, audit entry or delivery row. Only Management can register, change, rotate or test. Payloads carry no margin, cost, notes, payments or custom fields; a creator moved to Sales stops receiving other people's deal values. Deliveries are tenant-isolated: the job for one organisation sends none of another's, a delivery row cannot reference another organisation's endpoint (composite foreign key), and another organisation's endpoint cannot be read, rotated or changed.

Findings: W-1, H-1, I-1, I-3.

### 2.5 Custom roles (FIG-1188)

Read: `roleEditorService.ts`, `lib/roleLimits.ts`. Tests in `tests/g301ReviewRoles.test.ts`.

Held: the reserved list blocks payments, margin, audit, bulk export and every organisation-running key, by spelling, spacing, case, zero-width characters, encoding, empty and prototype-name tricks, and a role "based on Management" copies none of them. Extra body fields (organisation, key, system flag, creator) are ignored. Another organisation's custom role cannot be assigned. A member, including a holder of a broad custom role, cannot give themselves Management. The last-Management lock-out is covered by the builders' tests and was not re-attacked. **Crawl:** every `GET` route of the organisation (unknown ids and ids of the planted records filled in) was called as Management (control: the planted deal value, invoice amount, payment amount and catalogue cost are all readable), then as a custom role holding **every grantable key** with the deal value hidden, as a second Sales member, and as Delivery. No route failed with a 5xx. Only one route showed a planted value to a role that should not see it: R-1.

Findings: R-1, P-1, I-4.

## 3. What was not checked

- No browser, no live server, no Playwright; nothing was run against a real deployment, Vercel, or the platform's real proxy headers. Whether Vercel overwrites a client-supplied `X-Forwarded-For` (which decides A-1's severity) was not verified.
- Webhook behaviour against a real public receiver: redirect handling, the response-size cap and the idle timeout were read from source only (`ssrfGuard.ts`, no follow-redirect library, `res.destroy()` past the cap). No outside host was contacted.
- DNS: real resolution, split-horizon and IPv6-only answers. The resolver was always a stub.
- The crawl covered `GET` routes with default query strings and the ids listed in the test. It did not cover `POST`/`PATCH`/`DELETE` routes as the custom role, routes needing particular query parameters, files and signed URLs, exports as a custom role (the report export route for the monthly report is likely affected by R-1 and was not called), the assistant (`ai.use`) and chat.
- Cross-organisation probes in the public API covered the six entities, not every sub-route.
- Timing side channels (token lookup, 404 versus 410) were reasoned about, not measured. The token is looked up by hash of a 256-bit value, so a timing attack on the comparison is not practical; the 404/410 difference on a signing link reveals that a link existed only to someone who already holds a valid token.
- Denial of service and load: no load test; the rate limits were exercised by seeding counters, not by traffic.
- Database-level attacks that need the owner or superuser role (trigger disabling, `session_replication_role`) were used only to set up states, not as attacks.
- The V2 migrations were not applied to an upgrade path from V1.1 data; only the fresh test database was used.
- Everything outside the four areas (two-step sign-in, platform console, OAuth, mail sync, assistant, forms, cron endpoints) is out of scope and unreviewed here.
- The last-Management lock-out, role audit trail and cross-organisation role ids rely on the builders' tests (`tests/m17closeRolesB706.test.ts`), which were run and pass but not independently extended.

## 5. Reproducing R-1 by hand

Organisation on V2, one won deal worth 5,400,007 this month. Create a custom role with `reporting.view.all`, `leads.view.all`, `deals.view.all` and the field rule `deal.value: hidden`. As a member of that role call `GET /api/orgs/{slug}/reports/v2`: the "Won value" row of the table and the last bar of the chart carry 5400007 and the answer says `valueMasked: false`. Suggested fix, not applied: give MONTH_VS_LAST its money row a way to be hidden (for example mark the row, not the column, or compute `canSeeValue` with `canSeeAggregateValue(ctx)` and drop the "Won value" row and its chart point), and add the report to the masking matrix test so a new report cannot ship with `valueColumns: []` and money in a row.

## 6. Would I let this ship to a first customer?

Not yet, and the reason is narrow. The parts that most often go wrong in this kind of feature were built carefully and held up against what I could throw at them: the webhook address guard is more thorough than most (it re-checks the address on the connection that is actually used and refuses every spelling I tried), the signing link and the signed record behave correctly under races, replay and tampering, API tokens run strictly as their owner and stop at once, and the break-glass access is walled in at the database for every role, with masking done by not selecting the columns. The one result I would not accept is R-1: it is a plain breach of the rule that deal values are masked everywhere, it is reachable by an ordinary custom role in the default screen of the reports page, and it is a few lines to fix and one test to add. I would also want W-1 closed or consciously accepted (an endpoint should stop speaking when its creator loses the right to manage settings), and a decision recorded on P-1 (is bulk reading through the API acceptable, given the export rule). The Low and Info items can follow the first customer, except that `TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS=1` must be set in production, and I could not verify what the real proxy does with `X-Forwarded-For`. Because this review is a read of the code plus targeted tests by an AI agent, with no live system and no outside network, it lowers the risk but does not remove the need for Michael's approval or an independent penetration test of the public endpoints before the customer relies on them.
